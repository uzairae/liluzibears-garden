how we lost morality

"I must study Politics and War so that my sons may have liberty to study Mathematics and Philosophy in order to give their children a right to study Painting, Poetry, Music, and Architecture."
-John Adams (paraphrased)

          For the better part of our time on this planet, humanity has centered upon the innovation of new technologies and ideas. Great societies have grown and fallen on simple principles and inventions that have dictated the direction of life. However, in contrast with the modern era, previous societies have always accompanied their advancements with art. For example, DaVinci was not only an architect but also an artist. His inventions were created with not only an insight into the scientific aspect of them, but also with an interest in the pure beauty and natural harmony that had came out of them. In his so-called "non-artistic" line of work in engineering (as it would be viewed today), he took time to feel and connect on a personal level with his handiwork.
          This connection between artist and architecture, medicine and philosophy, and humanities and stem is not widely practiced in this day and age. People are expected to choose between either paths and become steadfast followers in that and only that. Through this seperation, innovation has become purely a means of production. Meaning, the point of innovation has become less about the advancement of humankind generally and more about the maximization of production. The way I'm wording this must make it obvious where this change has developed. The Industrial Revolution was a dramatic social revolution that has permanently reshaped the world (for better and for worse). Europe was completely reshaped in it's priorities and nothing was more affected than the practice of innovation. From this point on, the only purpose of innovating was to MAKE MORE MONEY!!! The deadly sin of greed truly encompassed the region as it passed through.
          As the prioritization of production and competition grew throughout Europe and the seperation between art and science became more prominent, the derealization of humanity grew. The attitude in the region disallowed the most foremost leaders to truly resonate with the arts and explore their own humanity. In these acts, the moral barriers of these leaders and scientists decreased, and immorality ran rampant. The growth of the production-oriented mindset led to these European powers to continue to compete with one another, growing globally around the world. In their blinded expansion, these leaders, disconnected with morality and displaying their developed utter lack of empathy brutally terrorized numerous ethnic groups and populations globally. The Industrial Revolution had managed to turn these humans' best interests away from humanity! Through their utter disregard for the wellbeing of other members of mankind, Europeans were able to ravage throughout the world and claim a large majority.
          Why didn't other regions of the world gain the control the Europeans did? Because other cultures did not make the decision to seperate art from science. They coexisted and grew together. In this way, morality prevailed. When morality is decrepit, nothing stops a man from destroying everything in his path. The empathy and respect that other cultures and civilizations maintained during this period is what led to their unfortunate colonization.
     In the modern world, we still see the ramifications of these processes. Empathy is extremely scarce and not common among many people. This is primarily do to the establishment that "if you want to survive, you cannot do art." By raising our societies in this manner, we are disallowing the development of self-exploration and natural empathy. Art cannot be exclusive of science. The only way that we can ensure our future generations make decisions that help humanity and allow us to perservere is if we allow them to develop a natural empathy instead of shutting it off.

          Always stick with art.
